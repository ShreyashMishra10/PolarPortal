About the Project

Why PolarPortal?

Polar science matters to every one of us, but most of it stays inside research papers. We want to change that.

Our Mission

The Arctic and Antarctic control a large part of Earth's climate. They store most of the planet's fresh water, reflect sunlight back into space and keep a record of the past climate locked inside their ice.

PolarPortal is an integrated outreach knowledge repository and media dissemination portal. It collects polar science in one place, explains it in simple language and shares photos, videos, podcasts and documents so anyone can learn from them and pass them on.

Our audience is school and college students, teachers, science communicators and anyone who is curious about the coldest places on Earth.

How we write: every article in the Knowledge Hub is written in our own simple words and is based on real, published research, such as papers in Nature and The Cryosphere, or official sources like NCPOR, NASA and NOAA. Each article names its source and links to it, so readers can always check the original science.

History

Milestones in Polar Exploration

From the first ships in icy waters to India's modern research stations.

1773

Captain James Cook's ships become the first known to cross the Antarctic Circle.

1820

The first confirmed sightings of the Antarctic continent are made by sailing expeditions.

1911

Norwegian explorer Roald Amundsen and his team become the first people to reach the South Pole.

1959

The Antarctic Treaty is signed, setting Antarctica aside for peace and science.

1979

Satellites begin continuously measuring polar sea ice, a record that continues today.

1981

India launches its first scientific expedition to Antarctica.

1983

India sets up Dakshin Gangotri, its first Antarctic station, and joins the Antarctic Treaty.

1989

Maitri, India's permanent research station in the Schirmacher Oasis, begins work.

2008

India opens Himadri, its Arctic research station at Ny-Ålesund, Svalbard.

2012

Bharati, India's newest Antarctic station, opens in the Larsemann Hills.

2022

India passes the Indian Antarctic Act to regulate its activities on the continent.

Why are the poles so cold?

Sunlight reaches the poles at a very low angle, so the same energy is spread over a bigger area. The bright ice and snow also reflect most of the sunlight back into space, and for months in winter the Sun does not rise at all.

Do polar bears and penguins live together?

No. Polar bears live only in the Arctic (north) and penguins live in the Southern Hemisphere, mostly around Antarctica. They never meet in the wild.

Who owns Antarctica?

Nobody. Under the Antarctic Treaty of 1959, Antarctica is used only for peaceful purposes and scientific research. Some countries have old territorial claims, but the treaty puts them on hold. More than 50 countries have joined it, including India.

Does melting sea ice raise sea level?

Hardly at all, because sea ice is already floating in the ocean, like ice cubes in a glass of water. The real concern is ice on land, in Greenland and Antarctica, which adds new water to the ocean when it melts.

How does polar ice affect India?

India has a long coastline with big cities like Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata. Rising sea levels from melting land ice increase the risk of flooding and coastal erosion. Changes at the poles can also influence ocean currents and weather patterns.
